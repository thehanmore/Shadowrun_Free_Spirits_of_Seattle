Recovered session recap

“Thumbs up.”

The runners leave Mr. K behind, but his mysteries—and their own new notoriety—follow them home.

11 August 2080 // 8 fragments recovered

The Security Room

The runners resumed where the previous session ended: in the abandoned underground security room beneath Southgate Mall, gathered around Mr. K’s decapitated body.

Pyrite noticed her own face displayed on a security monitor. Realising the camera system had captured the runners, she attempted to determine where the cables led and where recordings might be stored. Unable to locate the storage system or trace the cabling, frustration took over. She smashed monitors, tore out cables, damaged computer racks and wrecked the security equipment.

Pretty Boy returned his attention to the folded Joker recovered from Mr. K’s body. Four successes on his Assensing test revealed considerably more about the card’s magical nature, source and astral signature.

Cloak Explores the Tunnels

Cloak and Red left the security room ahead of Pyrite and Pretty Boy. Rather than immediately leaving, Cloak spent five to ten minutes exploring the surrounding subway tunnels, back rooms and service areas.

He discovered a large, heavily fortified steel door with structural bracing extending into the concrete walls. The door looked as old as the abandoned infrastructure. The security system beside it did not. A conspicuously modern biometric scanner had been installed next to it by someone with significant resources. Cloak noted the location without investigating further.

He also found a vertical ladder leading to a street-level manhole behind the abandoned station, providing another route into and out of the underground complex. The runners then separated, returned home and slept.

Debrief at Cheap Trix

The next day, Pretty Boy took the team to Cheap Trix for an informal debrief. He attempted to engage a Japanese businessman and his wife in conversation. The wife worked for a Renraku satellite company and offered information concerning the bridge massacre and Renraku’s conspicuous armoured deployment.

She suggested the incident might have involved an assassination attempt against a Renraku security chief. The chief died, and the situation became more complicated when DocWagon arrived to retrieve him under his medical contract. Renraku troops, unsure whom to trust, refused to surrender their colleague despite DocWagon’s contractual obligation.

The Bridge Aftermath

The confrontation escalated into a standoff. A Renraku soldier apparently panicked and raised his rifle toward DocWagon personnel. DocWagon responded immediately and lethally, shooting a significant number of Renraku soldiers.

DocWagon recovered its contracted client and extracted him. Personnel belonging to two major corporate powers had directly exchanged lethal force, leaving numerous Renraku soldiers dead and raising serious corporate, political and legal ramifications.

Grox Calls the Team

That evening, Grox asked everyone to come to the Circuit Breaker. The runners assumed he had another job. He did not. This was just a conversation.

Grox discussed the previous job and Mr. K’s fate, then asked Pretty Boy a pointed question: did he actually believe Mr. K was dead?

Grox raised the possibility that Mr. K’s existence as a technomancer complicated the obvious conclusion presented by a headless corpse. Perhaps Mr. K had escaped into the Matrix, abandoning or surviving beyond his physical body and continuing to exist virtually. Grox presented no strong evidence and reached no conclusion. He simply allowed the possibility to remain.

The conversation shifted to Arjun and the mysterious dark presence Pretty Boy had detected around him. Grox became considerably more serious.

Five Minutes to Talk About Mr. Dark

Grox activated a magical fetish for protection and a technological silencing field. The magical protection was temporary; they had about five minutes before the charm burned itself out.

Pretty Boy connected Mr. Dark to Mr. K’s fate, bringing up their meeting aboard the boat and suggesting the death followed Mr. K’s refusal of a deal. Pretty Boy appeared ready to suggest that Harlequin had subsequently killed Mr. K. Grox immediately stopped him and warned him not to say that name in the room. The precautions were intended to protect them from Mr. Dark and should not be assumed to protect them from the attention of the other individual.

Grox explained that Mr. Dark was not simply another enemy, powerful magician, criminal mastermind or conventional villain. He operated on another level: profoundly manipulative and Machiavellian, treating individuals, organisations and events as pieces in games whose objectives might be apparent only to him.

The runners were warned not to understand him through an ordinary conflict between good and evil. His actions did not require money, political power, revenge, ideology or victory. Mr. Dark was capable of manipulation, cruelty and evil simply because it amused or interested him.

When Pretty Boy repeatedly interrupted, Grox stopped him: “You cut me off again when I’m speaking, I’m going to put your head through the wall. Do you understand that, little mage?” Pretty Boy remained silent. After a pause, Grox added, “You can speak now.”

End of the Night and the Viral Video

With the serious conversation over, drinks were on the house. Pretty Boy stayed to socialise. Cloak had a couple of drinks, then went home to continue work on his Bat Cave. Red became thoroughly intoxicated and staggered home. Pyrite began planning travel to Tír Tairngire, hoping the Elven Free State might provide somewhere to lie low.

By then, the entire team had seen the viral footage of Pyrite and Red. Recorded from the dwarf’s phone, it showed Pyrite beating him before Red took the device and crushed it. Destroying the phone had not prevented the recording from escaping into the Matrix.

Smaller media and influencer channels accumulated tens of thousands of views. More seriously, Friends of Humanity seized upon the incident. One of its leading influencers took particular interest in Pyrite and Red, surpassing one million views within twenty-four hours.

Pyrite and Red’s faces were now being distributed to an enormous audience while a prominent anti-metahuman voice constructed its own narrative around them. The consequences had yet to fully emerge.
